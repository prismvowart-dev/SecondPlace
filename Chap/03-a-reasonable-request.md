# Chapter 3: A Reasonable Request

For three days, nothing happened.

People looked at him a beat longer in the hallways than they had before. A few first-years nodded at him now, the uncertain kind of nod you give a fact instead of a person. Kaito had taken to introducing him as "my roommate, Shindou, you might've heard of him," which Itsuki had asked him to stop doing four separate times, with a success rate of zero.

A girl from his methodology seminar cornered him after class to ask, with real desperation, which flashcard app he used. A guy two seats over in his language course wanted to know his sleep schedule, on the theory that some exact number of hours was doing the work. Itsuki gave both of them the same honest answer he'd have given anyone: he studied, a lot, most days, and there wasn't a trick under it. Both of them deflated a little and drifted off without following up.

Saionji herself he saw twice, once across a lecture hall, once in the courtyard, both times at the same unhurried pace as the day of the board, giving no sign that Thursday had stayed with her at all.

This lasted until the following Thursday, when he came out of his afternoon seminar to find her standing outside the door, arms folded, in a posture that suggested she'd been there a while and had no plans to pretend otherwise.

___

"Shindou-san."

"Saionji-san."

She said his name like she already knew what she was about to prove with it. "I have a question. I expect a complete answer."

"You can ask. I can't promise it'll be interesting."

"What is your method."

Not really a question. No room in it for the possibility he might not have one.

"My method for what."

"Don't." One word, and the hallway got noticeably colder for it. "You beat me by four points on an exam I have not lost since I was fourteen. I'm not interested in false modesty, and I'm even less interested in whatever this is. What did you actually do differently."

Itsuki considered this honestly, since she seemed like the kind of person who'd catch a dishonest answer immediately and hold it against him for the rest of the term.

"I studied."

She waited. Her arms stayed folded, but her grip on her own sleeve tightened, just slightly.

"That isn't an answer."

"It's the accurate one."

"Everyone studies, Shindou-san. I've studied for this exam since the semester began. I want the actual answer. Flashcard system, spaced repetition, a private tutor. I don't care what it is, I'd simply like it named so I can categorize it correctly and move on with my evening."

"There isn't a system. I don't have a tutor. I read the material more than once, in whatever time I have, most days, and I've been doing that for a long time. That's the whole answer."

"That's not an answer. That's a description of effort with no mechanism attached."

She didn't accept this so much as regroup around it.

"Some kind of interval system, then."

"No."

"A private instructor. Someone reviewing your work outside class."

"No."

"Some kind of memory system. The method of loci, a memory palace, anything structural."

"I don't know what that is."

Something in her jaw tightened at that, like the honest confusion had been worse than an outright denial would have been. "An application, then. Software. Something with an algorithm behind it."

"I use the notes app that came with my phone."

"A study group. Divided material, someone doing part of the work for you."

"I don't have time for a study group."

"Then what, exactly, do you have time for that apparently outperforms everything I've built."

"Nothing dramatic. I work most nights. I read in whatever's left, usually late, more than once. I've done some version of that since I was about twelve, because it was the only way to keep up with people who had things I didn't." He paused, aware this was already more than he'd meant to say, and finished anyway. "That's all of it. I'm sorry it isn't more interesting."

Something crossed her face at that, quick, gone before he could examine it. Her breath caught, just slightly, before it evened back out.

"That's not a real answer," she said again, quieter this time. "People don't simply work harder and produce results like that from nothing. There's always a mechanism. If there wasn't, that would mean—"

She stopped herself.

"That would mean what," he asked, since he was curious despite himself.

"Nothing." The word came out clipped, final. "It doesn't matter. I have what I need."

"You said my answer wasn't an answer."

"It confirmed what I needed it to confirm." She lifted her chin slightly. "Thank you for your time, Shindou-san."

She turned to go. Itsuki, against every instinct he'd built over eighteen years of not getting involved in things that weren't his problem, spoke anyway.

"For what it's worth, beating you was never the plan. I didn't know your name until the week I got here."

She turned back just enough to actually look at him, which she hadn't done once since the conversation started, and whatever was in her expression now was worse than the ice had been.

"That," she said, "is not the reassurance you seem to think it is."

He opened his mouth. Nothing arrived to fill it. By the time anything did, she was already three steps down the hallway, walking at the same pace as always.

Just before she reached the corner, she paused briefly, the kind of pause a person allows only when they think no one's still watching. He caught three words under her breath, low and even, delivered more like a diagnosis than a complaint. Something that sounded, from that distance, uncomfortably like *statistically insignificant*, though whether she meant the exam, him, or herself, he had no way of telling. Then she was gone.

___

Kaito had gone quiet during all of this. Itsuki noticed it before he noticed anything else. He waited until she'd turned the corner before making a sound like a kettle reaching full boil.

"Okay. I need you to walk me through what just happened, because from where I was standing, that was the single most terrifying conversation I've ever watched two people have without actually fighting."

"She asked a question. I answered it."

"You ruined her, man. I watched her recalculate in real time."

"I told her the truth."

"That's exactly the problem. If you'd lied, she could've boxed it up and moved on. Instead you told her there's no trick, it's just work, forever, with no ceiling. That's not an answer, man. That's a curse."

Itsuki turned this over as they walked. He hadn't meant to unsettle her. He'd believed the honest answer was the least offensive one available.

"Do you think she's okay," he said, before he could decide not to ask.

Kaito lit up like he'd just heard something he intended to bring up for the rest of the semester. "Oh, you're worried about her."

"I'm asking a question."

"You're worried. About Saionji Reina. Who, four minutes ago, looked at you like you were a problem she wanted to set on fire."

"I'm asking if she's okay."

"You've known this woman for one conversation, and you're already doing the thing." Kaito clapped him on the back, far too pleased with himself. "It's cute. I'm not going to let you forget it."

Itsuki didn't dignify the rest of it with an answer, mostly because he suspected any answer would make it worse, and partly because he didn't actually have one.

It hadn't felt like winning. It had felt, if he was honest, a little like breaking something he hadn't known was fragile.
