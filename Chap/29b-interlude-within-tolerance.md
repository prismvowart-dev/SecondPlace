# Interlude: Within Tolerance

*(Saionji Reina, between Chapters 29 and 30)*

The annex gym behind the humanities building opened at 5:30 to anyone with a student card, and was used, as far as Reina had ever been able to establish, by nobody before seven. That was why she had chosen it. It smelled of rubber mats and cold iron, and it had one working row of lights, which she had learned to switch on without waking the caretaker.

She arrived at 5:35 with her phone in the hand she was not using for the door. At 5:31 she had been on the footpath behind the dormitory, and at 5:31 the phone had said nothing.

*Latency. Within tolerance.* On Saturday it had been 5:34.

She set it on the bench beside the chalk bowl, face up, which she had not done in three years of training. Then she chalked her hands. For the first twelve minutes there was nothing to report. Shoulders, wrists, the slow lean forward until the floor was a long way down and her own weight was a figure she could read from her palms.

At 5:47 she came down from the third attempt, shook out her wrists, and looked.

Nothing.

*Concern. Mild. Situational. Resolving.*

She chalked again and went up again and held it for twenty-eight seconds, which was not thirty-three. She came down to a blank screen. The italic voice that usually supplied the next line did not supply it. She noticed that, and put it on the list of things she would examine later.

At 5:52 she took the store's number from the back page of her notebook, where she had copied it on Thursday in black ink, because it was only a number. She rang it with chalk on her thumb.

A man answered on the fourth ring, in the voice of someone who had been awake for eleven minutes. "Convenience, this is..."

"I am enquiring after Shindou Itsuki. He works the overnight."

"Shindou? He's gone. Left right at five, he's always right at five..."

"Did he say where he was going?"

"Home? He walks. Is something..."

"No," Reina said. "Thank you." She ended the call before the man could finish a question she would then have had to answer.

She rang Itsuki's phone. It rang through to the end and gave her a recorded voice. She rang again. She was aware that this was the point at which a person was supposed to feel something, and that what she felt was the blank she had felt in front of a results board in May, a number that fit no column.

She rang Yui.

It took seven rings. "It's ten to six."

"I am aware of the time. I need you to wake Nomura-san and ask him whether Shindou is in his bed."

A silence, in which Reina could hear Yui sitting up. Yui did not ask why. She said, "Hold on," and the line went dead, and Reina stood in a gym that smelled of cold iron and counted. At 5:56 the phone rang.

"His bed's empty," Yui said. "Kaito says it hasn't been slept in. His jacket's gone. Reina. What is going on?"

"He didn't text."

"He's twenty minutes late with a text."

"He is twenty-six minutes late," Reina said. "He has never been late by more than four."

She had the bag on her shoulder before she knew she had decided. She did not switch off the lights. At the door she looked at her hands, white to the wrists, and did not wipe them.
