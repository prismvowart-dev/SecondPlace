# Chapter 15: Kuze Shun

"Kuze Shun," Kaito said, with the air of a man delivering news he had been sitting on for hours, which he had. "Third-year, politics track. Same seminar line as you, which you'd know if you ever looked up from your own shoes. Two fellowship offers already, for after graduation. Captain of the kendo club. Last spring he tutored a first-year for free because the kid's family couldn't cover the seminar fee, and never told anyone. The kid told everyone."

"You found all that out in one afternoon."

"I have a gift." Kaito flopped back onto his bed. "Also Yui knows his lab partner. But mostly I have a gift. Oh, and allegedly he used to be a lot."

"A lot of what."

"Nobody would say. Just 'a lot.' The lab partner did a face." Kaito studied the ceiling with the expression of a man reviewing a map. "Point is, the guy's apparently just good. Not fake-good. Which is worse, from where you're sitting."

"I'm not sitting anywhere."

"Sure," Kaito said, in the tone of someone who intended to keep saying it until it stopped being funny, which at this rate would take a while.

___

Itsuki went to the mixer for a reason he could defend. The letter in the bottom of his bag said *demonstrated research output, as assessed by faculty evaluation*, and faculty would be at the mixer, standing by a table of lukewarm tea, evaluating. He put on the one shirt he owned that wasn't a hoodie and spent the walk across campus calculating how much of the evening it was going to cost him.

The function room sat two floors above the lecture hall, a long, low space with folding tables, paper cups, and a carpet that had given up on its pattern years ago. One wall was glass cabinets of trophies and photographs from the university's affiliated schools, mostly debate and kendo. Yamamoto was by the window, eating a biscuit with the stillness of a man who had done this forty times and enjoyed none of them and would never say so.

Reina found him within the first ten minutes, which he chose not to read into.

"You came."

"You said attendance was noticed."

"I said Yamamoto said that. I didn't expect you to actually come." She stopped. "I'm glad you're here."

She said it straight, the way she'd thanked him for the case, and he was still deciding what to do with it when he noticed the tea table. She had put the lavender cardigan over a charcoal dress and pinned her hair half up, and two second-years at the urn had stopped pouring. One of them went on looking at her while his cup filled and overflowed onto the cloth, and nobody told him.

Itsuki looked at the trophy cabinets instead, because it gave his face something to do.

The third one down held a photograph from the affiliated high school, six years old by the caption. A boy in kendo whites held a trophy against his hip in one hand, and with the other he held up a flat palm to the camera, as though refusing applause he considered his due. His chin was lifted at an angle people used when sitting for a coin. It took Itsuki a moment to match the name underneath to the man crossing the room toward them.

"Saionji."

No honorific. He hadn't worn the pin today, but the jacket was gray-green again, and the ink was gone from his cuff.

"Kuze-senpai," Reina said, in the voice of a woman who had since looked up his name.

"Glad you made it." A glance at Itsuki, brief and warm. "Shindou, wasn't it? The part I liked best last week was where you contradicted her in front of the room. Most pairs never do that. They'd rather be wrong together."

"It was a mistake."

"It was the most honest sentence in the hall." Kuze said it without ceremony, and Itsuki was annoyed to find that he meant it. He turned his attention back to Reina, though not all of it. "I wanted to ask you about the funding case. I've been circling the same question in my own thesis and couldn't find precedent that clean. Would you have twenty minutes this week? Not about tonight. Just the case. I'd rather hear it from the person who found it than reconstruct it badly."

"I could manage twenty minutes." She took a small planner from her bag, found the page, and wrote *Thu 4:00, Kuze, funding case* in black ink, the way she would have written down a dentist.

"Thursday, perhaps. The café by the west gate. It's quiet after four."

"Thursday works."

Itsuki noticed the ink. He had watched her use red on exactly one thing, and it hadn't been a dentist.

It was a reasonable request, put by a reasonable man, about a subject that was defensibly academic. There was nothing in it Itsuki could object to without sounding like exactly what he'd spent two years insisting he wasn't. So he said nothing. Silence had always been the safe option, and tonight it did not feel safe so much as like standing very still in a room that had started, slowly, to tilt.

___

"You've gone quiet," Reina said, later, when Kuze had drifted off to greet someone else and the two of them had ended up by the window with tea neither of them wanted.

"I'm always quiet."

"You're quiet differently tonight." She looked at him the way she looked at a flaw in someone else's citation. "Did he say something?"

"He said the best moment was where I contradicted you."

"That is not what I asked."

He didn't answer. She didn't push, though she went on watching him a little longer than the silence required, and he had the unwelcome sense that whatever he wasn't saying, she'd heard most of it anyway.

"He's a decent person," she said at last. She chose each word as though it might be checked. "I don't say that about many people."

"I know."

"That's all I meant by it."

"I know," he said again.

The tea cooled between them, past the point where either of them would drink it. Across the room, in the glass cabinet, a boy of sixteen went on holding up a hand against applause nobody was offering.
