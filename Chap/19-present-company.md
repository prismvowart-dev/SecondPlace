# Chapter 19: Present Company

The reading group met on alternate Tuesdays in a room too small for the number of people Yamamoto had decided belonged in it: second-years from the seminar track, the two third-years auditing from the neighboring lab, and, this fortnight, a scattering of faces Itsuki didn't know, drawn in by the rumor that the professor running it was, in his quiet way, good value.

"Today," Yamamoto said, without looking up from the packets he was handing out, "we're discussing methodology disputes. What happens when two researchers examining the same data reach it by different roads." He set the last packet in front of Reina and Itsuki without comment, which from him was the whole comment. "I understand some of you have relevant experience."

A ripple of amusement ran around the table. Reina did not look up from her copy. Itsuki decided that this was probably the correct response and copied it.

When the discussion came to precedent versus empirical modeling, the two of them answered it the way they answered most things now, one starting where the other stopped. Nobody in the room found it remarkable. Kuze, three seats down, watched it with his chin on his fist, attentive and unhurried, taking notes on nothing.

___

He caught Itsuki before he'd made it three steps from his chair at the break.

"Shindou. Do you have a minute?"

"Sure."

"The empirical piece in your Section 3. How you weighted the control variables. I've been stuck on something similar in my own thesis and yours is the cleanest version of the problem I've seen." He said it without ornament, like a man asking for directions.

It was, inconveniently, a real question from someone who had read the paper instead of merely hearing about it. Itsuki explained. Kuze listened, properly, and asked a follow-up that wasn't a nod in disguise.

"Why weight by cohort size and not by aid category?"

"Because cohort size is the one number the registrar doesn't round. The aid categories get binned by whoever's writing the report that year."

"That's a good reason." Kuze thought about it. "That's the sort of reason that only occurs to someone who has actually read the footnotes."

"Somebody has to."

Kuze's mouth moved. He thanked him, simply, and didn't turn the conversation toward anything else, and the whole exchange had been exactly what it said it was, which was somehow worse.

___

He had barely turned back to his own packet when Kuze's voice carried again, a few seats down now, lower, addressed to Yui, who was wedged into the corner with her phone and her cardigan sleeves pulled over her hands.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Depends."

Kuze glanced, briefly, toward the table where Reina and Itsuki still sat with the same open folder between them. "Have they always worked together this well?"

Itsuki did not look up from his page. He was aware, with the full, helpless attention of someone who has stopped being able to pretend he isn't listening, that the answer was coming whether he wanted it or not.

"No," Yui said.

A pause.

"But they don't know how not to."

Kuze said nothing for a moment. "I see," he said.

Neither of them said anything else. Itsuki read the same sentence of his packet four times and absorbed none of it.

___

"You're staring," Reina said, sliding into the chair beside him.

"I'm reading."

"You've had the same page open for several minutes. I've been watching you not turn it." She followed his line of sight, Kuze already drifting toward the coffee table, Yui back to her phone, the whole exchange folded back into the ordinary noise of the room. "He's probably asking about the room booking for next month. Yui handles half this cohort's scheduling whether anyone asked her to or not."

"Probably," Itsuki said, and didn't correct her, and found that letting the wrong answer stand cost him something he hadn't expected it to.

___

Yamamoto stopped him on the way out, which was rare enough that Itsuki nearly missed that it was deliberate.

"Shindou." He shifted the papers under one arm. "Section 3 was well done. The precedent case especially. It's unusual for someone in your line to go looking in the law library."

"I had a reason."

"I'm aware you did." Something in his voice moved, faint enough to miss if you weren't listening for it. "Keep going the way you're going. It's working."

"The research, or..."

"Mr. Shindou." Yamamoto didn't slow down. "I'm old enough not to answer questions I wasn't asked."

He was gone before Itsuki could decide whether that had been an answer or a very polite refusal to give one.

___

Reina left ahead of him, sliding her chair in with the firmness of someone with somewhere else to be, and it wasn't until the room had mostly emptied that he noticed her notebook sitting where she'd left it.

He picked it up to catch her before she got far. It fell open on its own, spine loosened by a term of use, to a page written in his own cramped hand, front to back.

Not a margin. A whole argument, laid out from beginning to end, from a night in November he only half remembered, when the problem had been interesting enough that he'd simply kept writing until there was nothing left to say. In the margin, in her upright hand, a single word: *Disputed.* And under it, smaller, in the same hand but a different pencil: *Correct.*

He closed it before he had read past the first line.

He carried it flat under one hand so the loosened spine wouldn't bend any further, caught up with her halfway across the courtyard, and held it out without mentioning that it had fallen open. She took it, thanked him absently, already thinking about something else, and was gone again before he had finished the sentence he hadn't said.

He watched her go for a moment longer than the moment required. Then he turned toward the dormitory, and the sentence he had heard four times that afternoon came with him.
