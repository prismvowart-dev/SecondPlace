# Chapter 1: An Unremarkable Arrival

The bus had taken eleven hours, and Itsuki had spent most of them awake, doing the arithmetic he always did when there was nothing else to occupy his hands: how many hours of sleep he'd lost, how many he could recover before Monday, and whether either number mattered next to the one printed on the letter folded into the outside pocket of his bag.

He'd read that letter enough times to recite it. He read it once more anyway, in the grey light before the driver called the last stop, because some part of him kept expecting the numbers to have changed overnight.

They hadn't. Tuition and dormitory fees, ¥580,000 a term, waived in full on one condition: standing in the top percentile of the cohort, confirmed fresh at the end of every term. Miss it once, and Koyo wouldn't expel him. They'd simply stop paying, and the difference between those two outcomes wasn't one he had any interest in testing.

He folded the letter back along its old creases and put it away.

The gate, when he reached it on foot, opened onto an avenue of cherry trees old enough that whoever had planted them clearly hadn't worried about a single yen of tuition in their life. It struck Itsuki as an efficient way to announce what kind of school this was before you'd even found the front hall. He appreciated efficient information. He was less enthusiastic about the trees themselves, which shed constantly and stuck to everything. He discovered this brushing a petal off his sleeve, from the inside of a bag he could have sworn he'd zipped.

A knot of new students had already stopped dead in the middle of the path to photograph one of the trees, a tree that had stood in that exact spot for decades and would go on standing there with no assistance from any of them. Itsuki walked around the knot and kept going.

The dormitory sat at the far edge of campus, plain and square next to the ornate stonework closer to the gate. It wasn't the kind of building that ended up in a brochure photo, though whether that had anything to do with who was paying full tuition and who wasn't, Itsuki couldn't say. He decided not to spend his first afternoon guessing. He didn't mind his half of it. The room had a bed, a desk, and a window that looked out on a bike rack instead of the trees the brochures liked to photograph, and according to the floor plan pinned downstairs, it sat an eleven-minute walk from the library. That was the only fact about the room he bothered to remember.

He'd barely gotten his key into the lock when someone shoved the door open from the other side.

___

"Sorry — wait, no, this is right, this is 214, are you 214?"

The door's owner was already inside by the time he finished the question: tall, dark-haired, dressed like he'd left the house at a run, looking at Itsuki with the open curiosity of someone who'd been waiting in a hallway with nothing to do and had run out of patience for it.

"I'm 214," Itsuki said.

"Great. Me too." He stuck a hand out like they were closing a business deal instead of standing in a dormitory hallway. "Nomura Kaito. I saw your name on the roommate sheet and there's no photo, so I've been extremely curious."

"Shindou Itsuki."

Kaito shook his hand once, hard, and glanced down at it on the way back, long enough that Itsuki noticed him clock the thin white line across the back of it.

"That from something interesting?"

"No."

"Cool. Mysterious. I respect it." Kaito was already moving past him into the room, inspecting it with the confidence of someone deciding where the furniture would go regardless of anyone's opinion. He dropped his bag on the bed nearer the window without asking, which settled the question of whose bed that was. "Full disclosure, I have zero plan for the next four years and I'm hoping some of yours rubs off on me. I checked the entrance scores downstairs. Yours is stupidly high."

"I studied."

"Sure. Everyone says that." Kaito grinned like the two of them were already friends and was simply waiting for Itsuki to catch up. "Anyway. Welcome to Koyo. I don't really do the slow version of getting to know people, so — congratulations, we're friends now."

Itsuki considered the sixty seconds that had just passed. In that time, he'd been shoved into via doorway, interrogated about his exam score, and assigned both a roommate and a bed configuration without a vote on either.

"Fine," he said, and meant it. There were worse ways to spend four years than next to someone who told you exactly where you stood instead of making you guess.

He set his own bag down on the side of the room that hadn't already been claimed and started unpacking: two changes of clothes, a stack of secondhand textbooks, a framed photo of his mother he set on the desk without ceremony. The scholarship letter went into the top drawer, under a folded map of campus, where he wouldn't have to look at it again until he needed to.

"What's your major, anyway?" Kaito asked, already flopping onto his own bed like the rest of his unpacking could wait indefinitely. "Mine's business. I picked it because the building's closer to the gym. Extremely rigorous selection process."

"Political science. Faculty of Law."

"Of course it is." Kaito said it like a man confirming something he'd already suspected. "You've got the exact face for it."

"What face."

"The one that reads legislation for fun."

Kaito narrated the rest of the afternoon out loud: the location of the nearest convenience store, a theory about the cafeteria schedule that appeared to rest on nothing at all, a running list of people he intended to befriend before the week was out. Itsuki listened without needing to add much, which suited him fine.

___

They walked to the orientation hall together, past a building with STUDENT RESULTS HALL over the door in lettering gone slightly green with age. Through the open doors, a glass-fronted board held a single sheet: this year's entrance results for the Faculty of Law, twenty names ranked in print small enough that the students crowded around it had to lean in to find their own.

"That's the thing your name's on," Kaito said, already walking past it. "They do this after every big exam, apparently. Whole school treats it like a shrine."

"That's a lot of ceremony for a sheet of paper."

"It's deeply unhinged and I respect it completely." Kaito glanced back. "You'll probably end up on there again, given the whole score situation. Good luck. I hear it gets competitive."

Itsuki looked at the board once. His own name sat near the top, a few lines under the one that opened the list: SAIONJI REINA. It meant nothing to him yet. The board itself was one more fact about how the school kept score, sitting next to the tuition deadline and the dormitory assignment in his head, and he kept walking.

The orientation hall was cavernous enough that several hundred first-years whispering at once produced something closer to a low mechanical hum than actual conversation. Itsuki found a spot near the back and considered it a solved problem. Kaito had other ideas, and had a hand on his shoulder steering him toward the middle of the crowd before either of them had finished the sentence about it.

Then he stopped walking.

Itsuki followed his line of sight and understood why a moment later. The crowd near the side entrance had done something crowds of eighteen-year-olds rarely managed on their own: it had organized itself. Not into a line. More like water finding the shape of a container nobody had told it existed, a channel opening through several hundred people, wide enough for one person to cross without brushing a shoulder.

The person walking through it was tall for a girl, though not dramatically so, with hair a pale, dusty pink left loose on purpose rather than out of laziness. Thin, precise glasses sat on her face like they'd always been part of it. She moved like someone who had never once had to check whether a room would make space for her, mostly because it always had.

She wasn't smiling. Her uniform was cut the same as everyone else's in the hall, and on her it read like an ongoing negotiation the fabric was quietly losing, one more reason people's eyes caught on her and stayed a beat longer than manners usually allowed, then darted away before they were caught looking.

"Okay," Kaito said, voice dropped to something almost reverent. "That's Saionji. Saionji Reina."

The name caught on something. It took Itsuki a moment to place it: the one that had opened the entrance list, a few lines above his own.

"Should that mean something to me."

"You are unbelievable." Kaito looked at him like he'd admitted to not knowing what fire was. "First place, three years running, middle school through high school. I heard she topped the mock exams so hard last cycle the runner-up transferred schools out of spite. Her family's some old academic name — mother or grandfather set a record here decades back, I don't know the details — and everyone's just assumed she'll do it again since she was about twelve."

"That's a lot to put on a twelve-year-old."

"Sure, but you don't look at her and think pushover." Kaito was still watching her cross the room, unashamed about it in the way only Kaito ever seemed to be about anything. "Third-years call her Saionji-sama. Half of them started it as a joke. I don't think it's a joke anymore."

It read like a character assembled for a novel rather than an actual person standing in an orientation hall: the name, the rank, a face that made strangers lose their train of thought. Itsuki had no reason to think any of it would matter to him. Rankings, in his experience, were a means to an end, proof you'd cleared whatever bar had been set in front of you. He'd never had the luxury of caring about one for its own sake. He'd only ever had the luxury of needing one.

He looked a moment longer than the situation required anyway. She didn't seem pleased to be the reason a room had gone quiet. Composed, yes. Pleased, not exactly.

It wasn't his business. He let it go and turned his attention to the front of the hall as the opening remarks began.

He didn't think about her again for the rest of the ceremony. If asked, he'd have said he could go the whole semester without thinking about her at all, and he'd have believed it completely. He had no way of knowing yet how much of it he'd get wrong.

___

By the time they walked back, the afternoon light had turned the cherry blossoms from pink to something closer to gold, and Kaito was still talking: a running tally of everyone he'd made eye contact with, a theory about which club had the best snack budget, a ranking of every convenience store bento he'd eaten in his life so far.

Itsuki let it wash over him. It didn't ask much of him, and after eleven hours on a bus and a morning spent learning his own social calendar from someone else, that was worth something.

Back in the room, with Kaito finally quiet over a game on his phone, Itsuki sat at the desk and pulled out his schedule. He'd already memorized it on the bus. One date on it he hadn't needed to memorize, because he'd circled it himself before he ever left home: the first major exam of the term, three weeks out, the one that would decide whether the letter in his top drawer stayed a promise or turned into a phone call to his mother he had no intention of making.

He pushed his glasses up without noticing he'd done it, the way he did whenever a problem needed more of his attention than his face was currently giving it, and looked at the circled date until the number on it stopped changing shape in his head.

Three weeks. He'd worked with less.

He turned off the desk lamp, and Koyo University's newest, least remarkable student went to sleep without giving a girl with pale pink hair, a board he'd glanced at once, or the three weeks now standing between him and the date he'd circled, another thought.

He'd have reason to think about all three eventually. Tonight, he didn't know that yet.
