# Chapter 30: Not Home

The first thing that came back was a sound. It was steady and mechanical and spaced a little too far apart to belong to anyone's heartbeat.

The second was the ceiling, tiled in small gray dashes he did not recognize from the dormitory, the store's back room, or anywhere else he had ever lain on his back and counted things.

The third was Kaito, pitched somewhere between casual and failing at it.

"Hey. Hey. Nurse. I think he's moving. Someone..."

Itsuki studied the dashes a while longer, found nothing he could use, and asked the only question that had assembled itself by the time his mouth caught up with his eyes.

"What day is it."

A silence with Kaito's shape. Then a short exhale, half laugh, half something that had been under strain for some time. "A man wakes up and the first thing out of him is the calendar. Okay. It's the third. December third. You've been under since the thirtieth. They kept you sedated the first two days, for the swelling, and before you ask, the doctor says it isn't a coma. I called it a coma twice and was corrected twice."

Three days. The number sat heavier than three days should. He tried to sit up, and his body vetoed the idea in several places at once.

"Don't," Kaito said, already half out of the chair. "He's going to want to redo the whole speech now that you're conscious. Hang on."

___

The doctor brought a chart, a penlight, and a calm that Itsuki recognized as engineered rather than earned. He took him through it at the speed of a man reading terms of service. At three minutes past five on the thirtieth, a car had struck Itsuki in the crossing by the school, the driver braking too late on an icy crosswalk in bad light. The tibia was fractured and had been pinned. Mild concussion. Two cracked ribs, nothing behind them.

"You were lucky," the doctor said, in the flat practiced tone of someone who knew it hadn't felt that way to anyone standing there.

"How much."

"I'm sorry?"

"The surgery. The room. The pin." His voice came out flatter than he meant it to, which was usually a measure of how far from flat he was. "How much is this going to cost."

Kaito's face did something complicated, like a man who had been dreading this question and found it less terrible, now that it was in the room.

"Already handled," Kaito said. "The driver admitted it at the scene. Told the police, then told his insurer. The claim was open before noon on the thirtieth. Surgery, the ward, the crutches, the physio, all of it goes to his policy. I made the guy from the insurance company say it twice, and then I made him say it in writing."

"All of it."

"All of it. You're not paying for any of this."

Something he had not noticed as tension let go of his chest, low and unglamorous, and was replaced at once by the next item in the queue. Six to eight weeks on the leg. No shifts. No seminar in person. He was doing the arithmetic on the scholarship paperwork before the doctor had finished the sentence about physiotherapy, and some detached part of him remarked that reaching for a spreadsheet before a feeling was probably not the correct order for a man who had just been hit by a car.

"You're doing the face," Kaito said, once the doctor had gone. "The one where you're three steps into a problem nobody gave you."

"Someone has to ask about the leave-of-absence deadline."

"Someone will. Later. Today your only job is being a guy who got hit by a car."

"Does my mother know."

"I called her the first night. She wanted to come right away, and I talked her into waiting till you were awake, so she wasn't just watching a machine. She's on the night bus tonight." Kaito's voice dropped out of its usual register. "She sounded scared, man. I didn't know her voice did that."

Itsuki put that somewhere to look at later.

"The store."

"Called them. They said take the time. Generous, if you ignore that the alternative was working with a metal rod in your leg." Kaito sank back into the chair, exhaling like a man who had held something up for three days. "You scared the hell out of me. I don't do the serious voice. I'm allergic. You made me do it for three straight days."

"Sorry."

"Don't apologize. Just don't do it again. That's the whole ask."

Itsuki let his head go back against the pillow. The ceiling dashes began, finally, to mean something other than unfamiliar.

It was on the third pass round the room, checking the corners the way he checked everything twice, that he saw the flowers.

They sat on the narrow sill under the window, arranged with a care that had nothing accidental about it. Placed once, adjusted twice. No card.

"Those yours?"

Kaito looked at the sill with an expression of enormous innocence that did not suit his face. "Wish I had that kind of taste. No idea. They were here when I came in yesterday."

"Who brought them."

"The ward clerk says someone left them at the desk early in the morning. No name. It's family only for the first seventy-two hours, so nobody else could come up, so, you know, they just left flowers. Very anonymous. Tall person. Or short. The clerk was hazy." He cleared his throat. "There was a thumbprint on the delivery slip. White. Like they'd been rock climbing. I don't ask questions."

"Kaito."

"I know nothing. I've been sworn."

"Sworn by whom?"

"By nobody, because there is nobody, because I have no information." He took out his phone and became absorbed in it at a speed that was an admission in itself.

Itsuki looked at the flowers a while longer, and did not ask again.

___

The phone was on the side table when Kaito went off to find a vending machine that wasn't broken. The screen had a crack in one corner and still worked. Most of the notifications were his mother and Kaito, as expected. One of Kaito's, stamped Wednesday afternoon, had clearly been typed in a corridor with one hand.

**Kaito:** *interview done. said nothing about packaging. they said theyll call. dont reply. just wake up*

Under all of it, in a single thread, were three messages, one a day, each stamped 5:30 in the morning.

**Reina:** *Late.*

**Reina:** *Late.*

**Reina:** *Late.*

He read them in order, the first of December, the second, the third. They were exactly as long as the word required. It took him longer than it should have to hold his thumb steady over the screen.

He typed *Not home. Hospital. Awake.* and looked at it. It was accurate. He sent it.

The three dots appeared and vanished. Appeared, vanished. It took her four tries to produce one word.

**Reina:** *Noted.*

A minute later, a second line.

**Reina:** *Do not attempt to sit up.*

He read that from a bed in which he had already tried, and failed, to sit up. Then he set the phone face up on the blanket, where he could see it.
