# Chapter 17: Logistics

Kaito appeared in the doorway on Saturday morning wearing the expression of a man who had made a promise months ago and just remembered it had a date.

"Yui's birthday is Wednesday."

Itsuki closed the book he hadn't been reading. "Okay."

"I told her in September, right after the thing at the south gate, that I'd 'do something good.' Those were my exact words. 'Something good.' Out loud, with confidence." He dropped onto the end of Itsuki's bed, which had stopped being surprised by this. "That was two months ago. I have a phrase."

"What have you thought of."

"A picnic. It rains constantly in November, scrapped. A surprise party, but she told me in March she'd hate one, which I now understand was a warning. Cooking her dinner myself." He held up a hand, thumb folded in. "I once set a kettle on fire. You were there."

"I was there."

"So I have three dead ideas and four days." Kaito looked at him with the face of a man asking for a favor he already suspected would be granted. "You're good at this. The remembering part. Help me not humiliate myself."

Itsuki considered pointing out that being good at remembering and having anything to show for it were separate facts. He went to find his shoes.

___

The shopping street near campus was crowded enough on a Saturday to make walking through it a small ongoing negotiation, and Kaito narrated the whole of it, as he narrated most things.

"Okay. What does she actually like. Concretely. Not vibes."

"You'd know better than I would."

"I know vibes. I need facts I can build a plan around." He chewed on it for a block. Then his face cleared. "Wait. Her umbrella. She complained in June, the wind turned it inside out on the way to class."

"That sounds right."

"So. An umbrella. A good one."

"The sturdy kind. It costs more, but it'll last past one typhoon."

Kaito nodded, already moving to the next problem before the sentence had finished landing. "And she said once she wanted somewhere with actual chairs. An evening. Somewhere quiet. She hates places where you have to shout to be heard."

"Then book ahead. Places like that fill up fast on a weeknight."

"Book ahead." He said it like a man discovering fire. "Umbrella. Quiet place. Book ahead. That's an actual plan."

"You already had it. You just hadn't put it in order."

"Sure, but you're the one who..." He stopped, seemed to reconsider what he'd been about to say, and settled for: "You're weirdly good at helping other people."

"We're shopping for Yui."

"We're shopping for Yui *right now*. That's not an answer."

Itsuki kept walking, which Kaito had learned meant the conversation had reached the part where it wasn't going to continue.

___

They found the umbrella in the fourth shop, a charcoal one with a steel frame and a price that made the scar through Kaito's right eyebrow climb with the rest of his face. He bought it anyway. They came out of a side street arguing about restaurants and nearly walked into Yui, who was standing at a crosswalk with a bag of groceries and the unbothered patience of someone who noticed things well before things noticed her.

She had a pastel cardigan on with the sleeves pulled over her hands, and she looked at the shopping bag Kaito was trying to angle behind his back.

"Nomura. What's that."

"Nothing. A thing. Not a thing for you, a thing for..." He looked at Itsuki with the panic of a man who hadn't prepared a lie. "Itsuki. It's for Itsuki."

"It's an umbrella."

"He needed an umbrella."

"I have an umbrella," Itsuki said.

"He has *two* umbrellas now," Kaito said, with the confidence of a man who had already lost and had decided to lose loudly. "Redundancy. It's a system."

Yui looked between them for a moment, deciding whether an obvious lie was worth dismantling in public. "My birthday's Wednesday," she said, mildly. "In case that helps with whatever this is."

"I knew that. I've known that since September, I made a whole..." He caught himself, visibly, and exhaled. "Okay. You got me. There's a plan. I'm not telling you what it is. It involves chairs."

"Chairs."

"A whole sit-down evening. The umbrella is a separate, unrelated gift that has nothing to do with the fact that your old one broke, which you mentioned once, in passing, and which I absolutely did not build an entire operation around."

Yui's expression didn't change. From Yui that was a verdict, delivered slowly. "Okay," she said.

"Okay?"

"I'll wear something that isn't for grocery shopping." She shifted the bag to her other hand, then paused, and glanced at Itsuki with the brief, assessing look of someone quietly revising an opinion. "You helped."

"He gave logistics," Kaito said, before Itsuki could answer. "I gave the emotional core. It's a partnership."

"Sure it is." She looked at Itsuki a moment longer. "Thank you."

"You're welcome," he said, and was mildly surprised to find he meant it more than the sentence required.

___

They walked back in the early evening, Kaito lighter than he'd been that morning, running through restaurant names with the manic energy of a man who had just discovered competence.

"I owe you," he said, somewhere past the second block. "Actually, not the joke version."

"You don't."

"I do. Two months of nothing, and then an entire evening in one Saturday." He kicked a stone off the curb and watched it skitter into the gutter. "If I ever get married, you're planning the proposal."

"Don't."

"I'm serious. You remember everything."

"I forget plenty."

"Name one thing."

Itsuki thought about it. "Laundry."

Kaito laughed, loudly enough that a couple across the street glanced over, and let it go. Itsuki didn't mention that he'd done his laundry that morning, before any of this had started.

"How do you even know what she needs," Kaito said, a block later, quieter. "Half of what you said today, I don't remember telling you."

"She tells you."

"...Really?"

"You just talk over the answer half the time." Itsuki said it without much weight, the way he said most true things about his friends. "I heard the part you missed."

Kaito had no comeback ready, which was rare, and they walked the last stretch in a quiet that was less comfortable than pointed. The shop windows were switching to their evening lights, warm and yellow against the early dark, and Itsuki's phone buzzed twice in his pocket.

He took it out under a streetlamp. It was his mother, who did not text. *Call when you can. Nothing urgent.*

He read it four times. In the whole of his life she had never once used the word.
