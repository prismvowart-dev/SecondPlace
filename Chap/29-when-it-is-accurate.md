# Chapter 29: When It Is Accurate

By Sunday the word had a shape.

*Home.* Friday at 5:31, typed with one thumb in the dormitory corridor. *Noted.* Saturday at 5:34.

**Itsuki:** *Home.*

**Reina:** *Noted.*

**Reina:** *Three minutes later than yesterday.*

**Itsuki:** *The light at the crossing runs long.*

**Reina:** *Noted.*

He had expected it to feel like a leash. It felt like a tide table, a thing someone else kept that told him when the night had officially ended. He caught himself, on Saturday, reaching for the phone at the register at ten past four, and put it back.

Kaito's message arrived at 11:48 on Sunday night, while Itsuki was lacing his shoes.

**Kaito:** *interview wed. rule one: dont say anything about packaging*

**Kaito:** *rule two: u good? not checking up. ok a little checking up*

**Itsuki:** *Fine. Good luck.*

**Kaito:** *insultingly short reply from a man who is definitely not fine but ok. eat something that isnt a rice ball*

He ate the navy bundle in the back room at twelve forty-five, standing up, since the crate was too low to bend to in the cold. Salmon, rice, pickled radish cut into thin even coins. Before one, as instructed. He rinsed the box in the back sink and set it by his bag, with the cloth folded in thirds the way she folded it, and then unfolded it and folded it the way he folded things, because the thirds were hers to keep.

___

The store was empty by one-thirty, the coolers cycling on and off with their hum, the fluorescent light doing its flat work over aisles nobody was walking. The old man came in at ten to two for his can of black coffee and the sports section.

"Still up," he said.

"Still up."

The taxi driver followed at ten past, same coffee, same silence. A second-year in a coat over pajamas bought batteries and a bag of tangerines, and apologized to the register for something that was not the register's fault. Itsuki rang him up. He restocked the rice balls, faced the shelf, and faced it again.

At twenty to four his manager's note was waiting by the till, in the cramped capitals of a man who had stopped being able to write in any other kind. *Dead tonight. Leave at 4:30 if you want. I'll cover the rest. Don't argue.*

He did the arithmetic standing at the counter. Thirty minutes at ¥1,300 was six hundred and fifty yen, about a third of the shoes he had been deciding to put off since October. He folded the note in half and put it under the till.

He stayed.

The old man came back through at ten to five. He did not buy anything. He stood by the door with his hands in his coat pockets and looked out at the lot.

"Mind the crossing by the school," he said. "The salt truck hasn't been. It's glass out there."

"I will."

"You don't. Young people never do." He pushed the door with his shoulder, and the chime went off behind him. "Still up," he said again, to the dark, and went.

___

At five Itsuki clocked out, wiped down the counter, and counted the till twice. It came out clean both times. His replacement was already at the back, pulling his apron over his head.

The cold outside had not decided whether it was still November. It was, for nineteen more hours, and it sat on his face like a flat hand. He pulled his collar up and set off the long way, the way he always went. Two blocks from the store, at the school, the road widened into a crossing.

He had a sentence. That was the odd part. He had been working on it since Thursday, between customers and in the corridor and in the shower, in the same quiet corner where he kept most of his arithmetic. *It's a smaller thing than something,* she had said, and he had said fine, and then he had gone home and understood that this one was something. Thursday, five o'clock, the table. He had the first four words. He was working on the fifth.

The light at the crossing was red. He stopped at the curb and shifted his bag to the other shoulder. The pavement ahead of him had a dull sheen to it, which in the gray before dawn could have been wet or could have been something else. The light was long. It was always long.

He took out his phone. 5:02. Twenty-eight minutes to be home, and accurate.

**5:03 AM.**
