# Chapter 4: Reconnaissance

The cease-fire held for about a week.

They passed each other some mornings outside the seminar room. She nodded once, the exact degree owed to a classmate and nothing more. He nodded back. They didn't mention the exam, the four points, or the conversation that had followed it.

It suited Itsuki fine. He'd never needed things resolved so much as he'd needed them to stop escalating.

Kaito did not share this view.

"This is a stalemate," he announced over dinner, apropos of nothing. "You two are just quietly agreeing not to look at an entire situation. That's not resolution. That's a ceasefire with no treaty attached."

"There's no situation to resolve. She asked a question. I answered it badly. We've both moved on."

"Nobody has moved on. I need intel."

"No."

"I haven't asked anything yet."

"You're going to. The answer's still no."

___

The cafeteria ran on a seating logic nobody had ever written down but everyone somehow knew: clubs at their tables, year-groups at theirs, the research-track first-years by the windows because Professor Yamamoto had once mentioned the light was better there and nobody had questioned it since. Itsuki had the map of it memorized within two weeks, mostly so he could avoid the middle of it.

He noticed her before Kaito did. That alone was rare. A girl with chestnut hair pulled into a loose half-ponytail, sitting alone at the end of a table, a tray of food she was rearranging more than eating, a notebook open in front of her that she kept writing in and crossing out.

Kaito noticed her about four seconds later, and Itsuki watched his roommate's whole body turn toward her like a compass needle finding north.

"That's her. Saionji's friend. I've seen them together six times, easy."

"No."

"I haven't said anything yet."

"You're wearing the face. I know the face."

Kaito was already moving. Itsuki followed, mostly to prevent property damage.

"Hi," Kaito said, arriving at the table like he'd never once considered the possibility of being unwelcome. "You're friends with Saionji, right? I feel like I've seen you around."

The girl looked up from her notebook with the expression of someone who'd clocked exactly what this was within about a second, and had decided to let it happen anyway.

"Tachibana Yui." She closed the notebook. "Long-suffering roommate, technically, though friend does most of the same work."

"Nomura Kaito." He sat down across from her without being invited, which she also allowed to happen, watching it with the mild interest of someone deciding how much rope to give a situation before pulling it back. "This is Shindou."

"I know who he is. Everyone does." Yui's eyes moved to Itsuki with more interest than they'd shown Kaito. "You're the one who broke my roommate's personality for about four minutes on a Thursday."

"That wasn't my intention."

"I believe you. Somehow that makes it funnier." She said it without any real malice, more like she was setting aside a good piece of information for later use. "She came home that night and reorganized her whole desk. Alphabetized things that don't alphabetize. I've lived with her six years. I know what that means."

"Six years?" Itsuki didn't have much to measure that against. Where he was from, six years put you back in elementary school, not a shared room.

"Since middle school. Koyo runs straight through from there, affiliated schools, dormitories and all. Most of this cohort came up through it together." Yui said it like a fact so ordinary to her it had never once needed explaining before. "Reina and I have shared a room since we were twelve."

Something shifted behind her eyes partway through the sentence, like she'd only just worked out where it was actually going.

"Which means most of us have known who she was since we were twelve, too." She studied him properly now. "You didn't, did you. Not until you got here."

He hadn't expected the question to follow him this far. "No."

"Huh." She held that a moment, an expression he couldn't quite place. "That's almost worse, isn't it."

She didn't explain what she meant by that, and went back to her rice like she'd said enough for one lunch. Itsuki considered asking her to clarify and decided against it, on the theory that the answer would only be a more articulate version of whatever Saionji herself specialized in not explaining.

___

Saionji arrived eleven minutes later, already talking before she'd fully reached the table. "Yui, I need the notes from Tuesday, you left before I could ask, and I refuse to reconstruct Professor Aihara's argument about—"

She stopped, mid-sentence, registering exactly who else was sitting there.

The silence that followed had a texture to it: four people simultaneously recalculating a situation none of them had planned for.

"Shindou-san," she said, with the careful, over-precise courtesy of someone determined not to let anything show. "Nomura-san."

"Saionji-san," Itsuki said, matching her tone exactly, since it seemed like the only available strategy.

"We were just talking about you," Kaito said, with the cheerful enthusiasm of a man who had never once in his life considered not saying the unhelpful thing out loud.

Saionji set her tray down with the exact, controlled motion of someone deciding not to make this into a bigger event than it already was. She sat across from Yui, and, by simple consequence of the table's geometry, at the farthest possible point from Itsuki, a piece of engineering she'd clearly worked out before she'd even reached the table.

"You could have just left," Yui said, not unkindly. "Nobody would have thought less of you."

"I have as much right to sit at this table as anyone."

"Sure. I'm just saying, most people, given the choice between their nemesis and eating standing up in a hallway, pick the hallway."

"He is not my—" Saionji stopped, recalibrating the sentence mid-delivery, and finished, with more precision than the moment called for, "he is not relevant to my seating decisions."

"Okay," Yui said, in the tone of someone who'd just been handed a gift and intended to keep unwrapping it slowly, over several weeks.

The conversation that followed covered a professor's grading policy, a club fair flyer someone had left on the table, and Kaito's ongoing theory about the cafeteria's rotating menu, which had no supporting evidence and several vocal opponents. Itsuki spent most of it aware, in the low-grade way of a man tracking weather, of exactly how far Saionji had chosen to sit from him, and how carefully she was managing to look like she hadn't chosen it at all.

At one point their eyes met across the table, over some comment of Kaito's that had made Yui laugh loud enough to draw looks from the next table. Saionji's expression didn't do anything dramatic. It flickered, just barely, into something like shared acknowledgment — *these two are ridiculous* — gone as soon as she registered he'd caught it. She looked back down at her tray. He did the same.

___

"That went well," Kaito said afterward, with the total sincerity of a man who'd apparently watched a different conversation than the one Itsuki remembered.

"She sat as far from me as the table allowed."

"Sure, but she stayed. If she actually didn't want to be there, she'd have invented an emergency and left. She sat through forty minutes of vending machine arguments. That's basically a declaration."

Itsuki considered this, and found, against his better judgment, that it didn't sound wrong.

"Also," Kaito added, with the deliberate casualness of a man who'd been building up to something, "Tachibana's number. In case you needed it. For group project purposes."

"I didn't ask for her number."

"I'm just saying I have it. Tucked away, for whatever situation requires a rapid exchange of contact information between friend groups."

"That's not a real category of situation."

"It will be," Kaito said, with the serene confidence of a man who'd already decided how the next few months were going to go.

___

The notification came the next morning, while Itsuki was still at the register during the dead stretch of his shift.

**[Yamamoto Seminar — Announcement] Term Project: Pair Assignments Posted**

He read the subject line twice, mostly because some part of him had already guessed, with the same low-grade dread he reserved for weather reports predicting exactly the rain he hadn't packed for, that a professor with that kind of sense of humor was unlikely to have left these pairings to chance.

He didn't open it until his shift ended.

He told himself this was because he was busy.

It wasn't.
