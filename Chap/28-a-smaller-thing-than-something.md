# Chapter 28: A Smaller Thing Than Something

The first frost came on a Thursday. By five o'clock someone had thrown salt across the library steps in a gritty fan, and it crunched under Itsuki's shoes all the way to the door.

Reina was already at the table. Her folders stopped at the center line, as they had not for weeks, and a strip of bare wood the width of a notebook had been left on his side. He sat down in it.

"You're on time," she said.

"You're earlier."

"I am always earlier. It is a discipline, not a comment on you."

He set a coffee beside the ruler. She moved a stack of papers six centimeters to the left. Under the table he heard the faint dry turning of her wrists, and neither of them said a word about it.

The research office had finally sent the income brackets, a spreadsheet of four hundred and eleven rows with the names stripped out. For an hour they did what they did. She filtered by column and read the results aloud in a flat voice, and he checked each one against the registrar's tables. The pattern was in the third column. Students in the lowest bracket, on full aid, left in their third year at twice the rate of everyone else.

"Twice," Reina said.

"Twice."

She wrote *2x* in the margin in red and underlined it hard enough to dent the page. He looked at the dent and did not say whom he was thinking of. She did not ask.

At twenty past seven she put her pen down.

"Nineteen," she said.

"Nineteen what."

"Two-fingered pushes. Since Monday." She said it to the notebook. "I stopped announcing the figures. I did not stop keeping them."

He took his hand away from his glasses. He had not known it was there. "That's invasive."

"It is a data set. You generate it in public."

"What does it say?"

"That your Tuesdays are adequate and your Thursdays are not." She capped the pen. "I have a logistical request."

"We agreed to tell each other when something was something."

"We did. It was vague." She squared the notebook against the table edge. "This falls short of that. It is a smaller thing than something. It is a procedure."

"What kind of procedure."

"When you finish at the store and you are home, you send me one word."

He waited for the rest of the sentence, the way she had once waited for his in this same room. It did not come. "That's all of it?"

"That is all of it."

"It'll be half past five."

"I am awake at twenty to six in any case." She said it to the ruler. "My mornings are not under discussion."

"I've walked home from that store for three years."

"I am aware of your qualifications. I am not asking about qualifications. I am asking for one word."

He understood it as arithmetic. Nineteen pushes, a Thursday that kept coming out worse than a Tuesday, a woman who preferred her variables accounted for. It was a reasonable thing to be worried about, and he had the sense not to say so.

"Fine," he said.

"Tonight."

"Tonight."

"Say the word."

"Home."

"Not yet. When it is accurate." But she took the small planner out of her bag, found tomorrow's page, and wrote *Text, 5:30* in black ink, the ink she used for the dentist.

Itsuki looked at it. He found that he did not mind ranking with the dentist.

"One condition," Reina said. "If it doesn't come, I'll ring the store."

"You don't have the number."

"I found it in four minutes."

"That's..."

"Research." She touched the bridge of her glasses with one fingertip, slowly. "I would prefer not to use it."

___

At ten to ten he packed up. Mori came along the stacks switching off the green-shaded lamps, stopped at their table a beat longer than the task needed, and moved on. At the door Reina held out the navy bundle without looking at him.

"The recipe yields six."

He took it. It was warm through the cloth.

"Eat it before one," she said. "Not at three, over the register."

"How do you know I eat at three?"

"Data."

He took the library steps one at a time, since someone had salted only the middle of them.

___

At 5:31 he stood in the dormitory corridor with his jacket still on and typed with one thumb.

**Itsuki:** *Home.*

The reply came before he had put the phone away.

**Reina:** *Noted.*

He read it once and noticed that the word was his. He had been sending it to her since September, in every size of silence. She had never texted it to him.

He took off his shoes, set the phone face up beside the pillow, and slept.
