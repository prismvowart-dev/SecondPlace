# Chapter 33: Sufficient Grounds

By the tenth of January he was back in room 214, upright on crutches for stretches of twenty minutes before his arms gave out ahead of his leg, and the visits had settled into something neither of them had named but both had started arranging their days around.

Reina came on a Sunday with her coat still on, which meant she had not planned to stay, and a folder under her arm, which meant she had something to report. She took Kaito's chair without being offered it and surveyed the room, one bed squared to the edge and the other buried under a snowdrift of laundry, the window onto the vending machine and the bike rack.

"It is smaller than Yui described."

"It's a room."

"It is a very efficient room." She set the folder on his desk and squared its edges, though nothing about it was crooked. "I spoke to someone."

He waited.

"A member of the Student Welfare Committee. My mother knows him, and he owed my family a courtesy." She said it flatly, as one reads a line from a contract. "I called it in. I attended the New Year gathering in full, four hours, seated beside a man who discussed golf, in return for one telephone call from my mother. She made it herself. I did not ask her to, and I do not yet know what I owe her for it."

"Saionji-san."

"I am telling you because the call worked, as far as it went. He agreed to read your file himself instead of leaving it in the general queue." She looked at the folder rather than at him. "He read it, and then he told me, to my face and with great courtesy, that there is nothing to be done."

"Because of the date."

"The hardship exemption must be filed within thirty days of the qualifying event. You are at day forty-one." She pronounced the number with distaste, like a mispronounced name. "He said the date is a bright line and that there is no discretion below the dean. The dean returned on the ninth. He had already seen the file and declined. I am quoting the committee member now, because I do not trust myself to paraphrase it kindly: the university's tolerance for exceptions ends where the exception begins to cost them anything measurable."

Itsuki thought about it. It was, as sentences went, well built.

"You didn't have to do any of that."

"I am aware I did not have to." She took her hand off the folder. "I did it. I would like that to stand as a fact, and not as something you feel obliged to thank me for."

"Thank you."

"I said not to."

"I heard you."

She almost smiled. It was there and gone, one corner of her mouth, and for a minute the room held something almost ordinary: two people, a desk, a folder that had fixed nothing, and neither of them pretending it had.

___

The letter came four days later, in an envelope thin enough that he knew what it was before he had it open. It was addressed in the font universities keep for decisions rather than invitations, and he read it twice, standing in the dormitory hallway because he had not yet made it back to the desk, one hand on a crutch he was supposed to be putting his weight through evenly and wasn't.

*...in light of the absence exceeding the threshold under Section 4.2, and in the absence of an approved hardship exemption filed within the required period, the Office of the Registrar regrets that continued enrollment for the current term cannot be sustained. Scholarship funds are administered on a term basis and are accordingly suspended with immediate effect. As scholarship coverage is applied retroactively on confirmation of enrollment status, tuition and associated fees for the current term, totaling ¥580,000, are no longer covered and are payable in full within fourteen (14) days of the date of this notice. Failure to remit payment within this period will result in automatic administrative withdrawal and referral to the university's collections process...*

There was more. A paragraph about reapplication after two terms. A paragraph about transcripts. A signature block with a name he did not recognize under a title that made it clear no single person had decided this, that it was only what several forms had added up to once they arrived in the wrong order.

He read the number again. It was a weight his hands understood before the rest of him did, measured in shifts he had not worked and could not now make up. Four hundred and some hours at the register, assuming he could stand behind one by Thursday. He did not have it. He would not have it in fourteen days however he arranged the arithmetic, and arranging arithmetic was the one thing he still did competently, in a hallway, on a bad leg, with nothing left to negotiate.

He noticed that the word *insufficient* was not in the letter. He had supplied it himself, the way he supplied most conclusions before anyone made him say them aloud.

Kaito found him ten minutes later, in the same spot, letter in one hand, crutch under the other arm, the weight still resting wrong on the leg.

"University?"

Itsuki held out the letter and watched Kaito read it. He watched his face do what it did when the joke would not arrive in time to help, and do something worse at the number.

"Fourteen days." Kaito said it as if checking that he had read it correctly. "They let you lie in a bed for six weeks and then give you fourteen days to find five hundred and eighty thousand yen."

"That's the policy."

"That's insane. That is what that is." He folded the letter once, the way you fold something you do not want to look at and cannot throw away. "Okay. Okay, so I have some saved, not much but some, and Yui would..."

"No."

"I haven't finished the sentence."

"I know what the sentence is." Itsuki shifted his weight onto the crutch properly, more for something to do with his hands than out of concern for his form. "I'm not taking a debt from you or Yui to postpone a decision that's already made. The money doesn't exist in fourteen days. It won't exist in twenty, or in two terms, or in whatever timeline lets me pretend this ends differently if I hold out a little longer."

Kaito went quiet in a way that did not suit him. "Does Saionji know?"

"Not yet."

"Itsuki."

"After. I'll tell her after. She'd draft a clause."

"That is the exact thing you do."

"I know."

Kaito looked at him a while longer, and let it go, which had become over the last six weeks his most dependable gesture. "So what. You just let them cross your name off."

"No." Itsuki looked at the letter in Kaito's hand and felt something settle, not relief, but the knowledge of which decision was actually his to make. "I'm not going to let them do it for me."
