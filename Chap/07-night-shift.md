# Chapter 7: Night Shift

Wednesday nights belonged to the coolers. Nothing else moved much after midnight. The drink cases cycled on and off behind their glass doors, humming at a pitch Itsuki had stopped noticing by his second week, and the fluorescent tubes buzzed faintly in the gaps between hums, audible only when everything else went quiet enough to let them through.

The regulars were few and never changed. A delivery driver came through around midnight for the same can of black coffee, paid in exact change without being asked, and left without a word past the receipt. The old man came at ten to two for the sports section, said "Still up" the way other people said hello, heard the same back, and considered the exchange complete for the night. Itsuki had learned the whole rhythm of it inside two weeks: which hour to restock the rice balls, which hour nobody came in at all, which hour the manager's cousin might wander through to check the register for reasons nobody had fully explained to him.

He was on his knees restocking the magazine rack, mostly gossip weeklies, which moved slower than anything else in the store and which he privately suspected existed only to fill the shelf space, when the door chime went off at 1:47.

"—and I'm just saying, an intervention is a reasonable response to someone reorganizing the same desk twice in one night."

"It wasn't the same desk. The second time I did the drawers."

He knew the second voice before he'd turned around all the way, and spent the last quarter-turn hoping he was wrong about it.

He wasn't.

Saionji stood just inside the door in an oversized lavender cardigan pulled over a plain white t-shirt, sleeves dragged down past her wrists like she'd put it on without checking the fit. Her hair was down past her shoulders, none of the discipline it usually held at the library table, and her glasses sat a fraction crooked, like she'd pushed them up in a hurry and hadn't noticed. She looked like someone who hadn't planned on being seen tonight at all.

Beside her, Tachibana Yui was already halfway down the snack aisle, moving with the unhurried confidence of someone who'd done this exact walk-through before. A small mole sat under her left eye, and her sweater sleeves were shoved up past the elbow, like she ran warm no matter the hour.

He got up off the floor and went behind the counter, since it was the only place in the store where he knew exactly what to do with his hands.

"Shindou-san." Saionji said it carefully, like she was still deciding what tone the sentence should take.

"Saionji-san. Tachibana-san."

"Oh, hey!" Yui looked up from the freezer case, delighted in a way that seemed to cost her nothing. "Reina, look at this. Actually look."

"I'm looking."

"I don't think you are. I think your brain needs a second to catch up." Yui turned back to him, openly curious now. "How long have you worked here?"

"Since the second week of term."

"Every night?"

"Most nights. One to five."

"Every night," she repeated, testing the number for weight, then let it go and reached for two ice cream bars instead. "This is the only place in a two-kilometer radius open past midnight. I checked before we left, so don't even start."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were about to."

Yui leaned over the counter to read the name stitched onto his polo. "Tanaka?"

"Borrowed. They haven't ordered mine yet."

"It suits you."

"It doesn't."

Saionji still hadn't come past the second aisle. Under the store's flat white light, which made everyone look faintly bruised at that hour, she was studying him the way she studied a page of someone else's proof: the smudged glasses, the shadow under his eyes, the polo that clearly belonged to a wider set of shoulders than his.

"You said you read the case study on your break," she said, finally.

"I did."

"This break."

"There's only the one."

She took a step closer, doing the arithmetic he could see forming behind her eyes. "One to five. And you were at the library until ten."

"Most nights."

"And you slept—"

"Two hours. Give or take."

"And the diagnostic. The one that put you on the board." Her voice had lost some of its usual precision, like the sentence was arriving faster than she meant to let it. "You studied for that on the same schedule."

"In the gaps. Same as everything."

She didn't say anything for a moment. Her hand came up and pushed her glasses back into place with one knuckle, a small, steadying motion he recognized because his own hand made it a dozen times a day without his permission.

"I assumed," she said slowly, "that whatever you were doing to place above me cost you less than this."

"It doesn't cost anyone nothing. I don't know why people keep expecting it to."

"That's not the same thing as what I said."

"No," he agreed. "It isn't."

Yui broke the quiet by setting two ice cream bars, a rice ball, and a can of the sweetened coffee Itsuki privately thought was undrinkable onto the counter, with the cheerful haste of someone changing the subject by putting something between everyone's hands. "We'll take these. I'm buying, before this gets any weirder than it already is."

He rang them up. His hands, he noted with something like professional pride, didn't shake. "Six hundred forty yen."

Saionji had her wallet out before Yui could reach for hers, which he was starting to understand was consistent with everything else about her. She counted the coins into the tray one at a time, largest to smallest, the edges lined up in a neat row, not looking at him while she did it.

Her eyes dropped to his hand where it rested flat on the counter, waiting for the machine to finish, and stayed there long enough that he noticed her noticing.

"What happened to your hand."

"Old."

He didn't offer anything further, and for a second it looked like she wasn't going to let it go. She usually didn't, not with a number that failed to add up clean. This time she did. She took the receipt, folded it once, and put it in her cardigan pocket rather than the bag, which struck him as an odd place to keep it, though he didn't say so.

"Thursday," she said. "Five."

"Thursday."

She left first, already through the door before Yui had finished gathering the bag. Yui lingered a moment at the frame.

"Kaito says you barely sleep."

"He exaggerates."

"Does he?" She studied him the way you study a house with its lights on at three in the morning, not unkindly, just noting it. Then the chime went, and she was gone too.

The door had barely settled when it chimed again.

"Still up," the old man said, already heading for the coffee.

"Still up," Itsuki said back.

When the store had emptied out again, he looked at the tray: six hundred forty yen, largest coin to smallest, every edge lined up with the next, exactly as she'd left them. He put the coins away one denomination at a time, in the same order, and told himself this didn't mean anything.

He was aware, even as he thought it, that he didn't believe himself.
