# Chapter 26: An Unscheduled Absence

Thursday, five o'clock, the radiator clanked on schedule, and Itsuki claimed the table by the window the way he had claimed it for the better part of two years, out of habit rather than urgency. He unpacked in the order he always unpacked. Notebook, dictionary, the pencil that had never made its way back into his own case. He opened to the page where he'd stopped and read the first line twice before it occurred to him that he was reading it twice because there was nothing yet to interrupt him.

Five-ten came. Five-fifteen.

She was, as a rule, there before him, and had been for months, long enough that her absence from the table registered the way an object registers by not being where a hand expects it. He didn't think much of it. Yamamoto sometimes kept the research track late on Thursdays. She hadn't mentioned it, but she didn't always.

He got through four pages before his phone buzzed against the wood, face down, the way he kept it during sessions as a courtesy neither of them had ever discussed.

**Yui:** *She's not coming today.*

He turned it over properly.

**Itsuki:** *Is she alright.*

**Yui:** *Depends what you mean by alright. Nothing's broken. She withdrew for the week. Left this morning.*

**Itsuki:** *Withdrew.*

**Yui:** *Leave of absence. A week. Officially "family circumstances." That's the version the office has.*

**Itsuki:** *And the version that isn't the office's.*

**Yui:** *Pick one. There are three going around already. Mine's the miai. Her mother phoned last night. A Wednesday. She only ever phones on the first Tuesday.*

He read that line longer than its length required.

**Yui:** *A car came for her. Black. Driver in gloves, standing by the door the whole time. I saw it once before, at middle school graduation. It isn't the kind of car that picks up a student.*

**Itsuki:** *Did she say anything.*

**Yui:** *Not to me. Going by the face she had on walking out, not to you either. It was the face she gets when she's already decided something and doesn't want an argument about it. You know the one.*

He did know the one. He had seen it across a glass-fronted results board, and again at a plaque case outside a seminar room, and each time it had meant she had finished a conversation with herself before anyone else got the chance to have it with her.

**Itsuki:** *Thank you for telling me.*

**Yui:** *Don't thank me yet. The table's yours for a week. Try not to talk to it.*

He set the phone down, face up this time, and looked at the empty half of the table without pretending to read.

___

The days that followed were unremarkable on the surface. He worked the shifts he always worked. He sat the seminar he always sat, in a room that felt larger with one chair conspicuously empty. He ate dinner across from Kaito twice and said little, and Kaito asked nothing, and Itsuki understood distantly what the silence was doing for him.

What wasn't unremarkable happened mostly at one in the morning, in the gaps between customers, standing at a register with nothing to do except face a cigarette case that didn't need restocking.

He thought about the rumor first, because it was the easiest piece to hold and the ugliest to put down. A miai. *There is a family matter to settle*, she had said, in a library in the autumn, and he had set it somewhere out of his way because at the time it hadn't been his business. It felt like his business now. He turned that over with some discomfort, because he had no standing to feel what he was feeling about it, none he could have defended aloud to anyone, least of all to her, and the feeling arrived anyway, uninvited and indifferent to whether he'd earned it.

He thought next about the whole shape of the last two years, slowly, the way he'd once worked through a proof he did not trust himself to have gotten right the first time. The results board, and her face going blank before it refolded. A library table, claimed before either of them agreed it was shared. A dictionary she'd been wrong about, and a pencil box that arrived with no explanation. A convenience store at 1:47 in the morning, and the quiet of being seen exhausted by someone who had not been supposed to see it. A photograph of two names circled in red ink, handed to him across the same table, undefended, because she had decided he deserved the accurate version instead of the polite one.

He had taken all of it in at the time. He had not, until a slow week with nothing to do but face a cigarette case, sat down and asked himself what it added up to.

It added up to more than he had ever let himself total in one sitting.

He was in love with her. He turned the sentence over the way he'd have turned over any conclusion he had finally permitted himself, checking for the error he half expected to find, and found none. It had probably been true longer than the week he'd spent confirming it. He no longer thought the trick had ever worked. He had simply gotten very good at closing a drawer before he had to look inside.

He drafted a message twice that week and deleted both before sending either. The first read *hope the week goes fine.* The second was three attempts at a sentence that kept sounding, on rereading, like a question he had no right to ask a woman who hadn't offered him the answer. He told himself both times that it would be inconsiderate to intrude on a family matter that wasn't his. He suspected both times that this was not the whole reason, and that the whole reason had more to do with what he'd worked out at one in the morning than with any concern for her privacy.

He did not send either. Handling it alone was, after twenty years of practice, the only shape the impulse knew.

___

By Monday he had stopped drafting and begun deciding. Not a confession. He still had no version of that sentence he could stand behind, and the memory of Kuze at the mixer, asking for twenty minutes in a clear voice as though it cost nothing, kept surfacing as a standard he did not yet know how to meet. Something nearer to honest than anything he'd offered her so far. A direct question, asked once, properly, instead of a fact left to gather interest in the dark.

He didn't yet know what shape the conversation would take. He knew only that there would be one, on his terms this time, and that he was done letting a drawer do the work his mouth kept refusing.

___

Tuesday came, five o'clock, the radiator clanking on schedule. He had bought two coffees on the way in, as he had every Tuesday and Thursday for months, the habit deeper than the thought behind it, his hand reaching for the second cup at the counter before the rest of him had caught up with why.

He set both cups on the table by the window and pulled out the chair across from his own, the one that had sat correctly upright and empty since Thursday.

He looked at the second cup for a while.

Then he drank it himself, slowly, both of them, sitting alone at a table built for two, and did not let himself feel anything about it he could have put a name to.
