# Chapter 2: The Board

The first month went the way Itsuki generally preferred a month to go: without much happening to him personally, and without anyone paying him more attention than a new face in a lecture hall usually got.

He got the job at the convenience store two days after orientation, an overnight shift a ten-minute walk from the dorm, the kind of hours a manager filled without asking too many questions about why a first-year wanted them at all. He clocked out at five, walked back through a grey campus that hadn't woken up yet, slept until eight, and made it to his morning lecture looking, by his own generous estimate, only moderately like a man running on four hours. Kaito noticed the pattern within the first week and asked exactly one question about it: "You good?" Itsuki said yes. Kaito accepted that and didn't ask again, though he complained about the alarm on a regular basis.

Kaito, for his part, spent most of April trying to drag him to things: a club fair, a first-year mixer that meant karaoke until two in the morning, a "quick" dinner Kaito swore would take forty-five minutes and that Itsuki had already learned to translate as at least three.

"You're going to burn out," Kaito told him more than once, in the confident tone of a man citing a theory he'd never once tested on himself. "Four hours of sleep a night isn't sustainable, man."

"It's temporary."

"You said that in April."

"It's still temporary."

He studied in the gaps: twenty minutes before a shift, an hour after one, most days at a table on the library's third floor, by the window, where the afternoon light came in at a decent angle and a radiator underneath clanked once around four like it was clearing its throat. He'd more or less claimed the table by default, since it was usually empty on a weekday and nobody seemed to want the noise. Once or twice he noticed someone else had been using it too: a receipt folded as a bookmark, a mechanical pencil lead he was fairly sure he hadn't bought. Their hours never overlapped, and he didn't think much more about it.

He didn't cram the night before the first major exam. Cramming, in his experience, was a luxury for people who hadn't already spent a month doing the work in small pieces, and he'd never had that luxury. He slept his usual four hours, worked his shift, and sat the exam with the calm of someone who already knew what he'd put into something, regardless of what the grade said about it afterward.

___

The results went up six days later, on a Thursday, and Itsuki and Kaito heard the crowd before they saw the board itself. It was louder than the hush that spot usually got, pitched with something closer to gossip than quiet reading.

"Wait, who?"

"Shindou. First-year, I think."

"No way she's actually second."

Itsuki caught pieces of it rounding the corner, before Kaito's hand landed on his shoulder and pulled him the rest of the way in.

The board looked the same as always: top twenty names, taped behind glass. Finding anything in it took longer than usual with this many people packed in close. Itsuki started around the middle of the list, roughly where he'd have guessed himself if anyone had asked beforehand, and didn't get far before a different name stopped him first, two lines above where he'd been looking.

**2. SAIONJI REINA**

He knew the name from two weeks of Kaito treating it like scripture. Saionji Reina didn't place second. As far as Itsuki could tell, she'd been sitting at the top of that exact board for three years running. Seeing her two lines down from there made him look up instead of down.

**1. SHINDOU ITSUKI**

He read it twice. Not because the print might change, but because some part of him had apparently sorted first place into the category of things that happened to other people, the same category as winning a raffle, and it took a moment to move the information somewhere more accurate.

Kaito recovered his volume at roughly triple the normal setting, which under the circumstances reached most of the hallway. "Shindou! You're number one—" Both hands landed on Itsuki's shoulders. "Do you get what this means?"

Enough heads turned that a few of them stayed turned.

"It means I answered more questions correctly than other people did," Itsuki said, mostly for Kaito's benefit, though it clearly wasn't landing there either.

"It means you just beat Saionji Reina, is what it means—"

A guy Itsuki didn't recognize leaned in, bright with the kind of energy that assumed proximity might rub off on him. "You're Shindou? That's insane, man. Congrats."

Someone else said "Congratulations" from a step back, flatter, the word costing him visibly more to produce. It took Itsuki a second to work out why. If Saionji had dropped to second, somebody had been quietly counting on second place for himself, and that somebody was currently looking at third instead, doing his best to sound glad about it.

___

Saionji arrived a few seconds later. The crowd did its usual trick of not being in her way by the time she got there, and she read the board the way Itsuki imagined she read it every time: fast, already three steps into whatever came after.

Then she stopped being fast.

Her expression didn't so much change as pause, the way a machine pauses when it's fed an input nobody built it to handle. Her eyes went from the number to the name printed above it, then followed the direction half the hallway couldn't stop glancing toward. She found him without much trouble.

"You're Shindou-san?" It came out less like a real question than a formality. She'd clearly already worked out the answer from the way people kept looking at him, and simply wasn't going to say the next part until she'd heard it confirmed out loud.

"I am."

"Congratulations." She said it to a point somewhere past his shoulder rather than to him directly, in a voice so level it made him suspect the exact opposite of calm.

"Thank you," he said, since nothing else came to mind.

She looked at the board once more, one clean pass, as if confirming the numbers hadn't quietly rearranged themselves while she wasn't looking. Then she took a small planner from her bag, wrote something in it without any change to her expression at all, and put it away.

She left at the pace she'd arrived at, through a crowd that parted for her going out exactly as readily as it had going in. A girl near the front, dark-haired, watchful in the way of someone who'd had years of practice keeping an eye on exactly this one person, peeled off and fell into step behind her, saying something too quiet to catch. Saionji didn't answer.

Kaito waited until she'd turned the corner before he let himself react properly. "Okay. Okay, that was— did you see her face? For about—" He stopped, apparently unable to find a unit of time small enough, and gave up on the number. "She looked like she'd forgotten how her own name worked."

"I saw it."

"You beat Saionji Reina. On your first real exam here. That has never happened, not once, not in three years—"

"I heard you the first time."

"You don't seem impressed with yourself."

Itsuki considered this honestly, since it was a fair thing to point out. He hadn't expected triumph, the clean kind that comes from confirming the work had paid off. What sat with him instead was a low, unwanted curiosity about the crack he'd just watched happen, and then not happen, in her expression, and underneath that, something closer to unease than he had a good explanation for.

"I don't think I did anything to her," he said, half to himself. "I just did the work."

"Yeah, well." Kaito clapped him once on the shoulder, gentler than his usual clap. He didn't say anything else about it, and didn't need to. "Sometimes that's enough to knock somebody's whole worldview over. You didn't have to mean it."

___

The crowd was mostly thinning by then, but a man in a loose button-up, glasses pushed up into greying hair, had stopped a short distance off with a folder under one arm, running a finger down a second sheet of paper as he checked it against the names on the board. He didn't look like a student. He looked like someone whose job for the afternoon involved exactly this list, and who had done the same task often enough before to find nothing urgent about it.

"Shindou Itsuki," he said, mostly to himself, checking the folder once more. Then, after a beat: "Interesting."

He didn't elaborate, and he didn't look up, and by the time Itsuki thought to ask what exactly was interesting about it, the man had tucked the folder back under his arm and wandered off down the hall.

"Who was that," Itsuki asked.

Kaito considered the question with more gravity than it deserved. "No idea. Faculty, probably, given the folder. Kind of gives off 'unnamed supporting character in someone's light novel' energy, not gonna lie."

He was already turning to go when something shifted behind his eyes, slow and complete, the look of a man arriving at a conclusion he found far too satisfying.

He turned back with the expression of someone who'd just solved a very old crime.

"...Wait."

"What."

"If he's the supporting character," Kaito said, with the deliberate patience of a man laying out a case, "that makes you the protagonist."

Itsuki gave him the flat, tired look reserved for people who had clearly been reading too much of something. "...What."

"I need you to stop reading whatever you've been reading."

Kaito, undeterred, only nodded to himself, the slow, satisfied nod of a man who'd glimpsed a great truth and intended to sit on it, and didn't bring it up again.

___

By dinner, the story had gone around most of the first-year class the exaggerated way stories do when they involve a number changing next to a name everyone already knew. Itsuki heard three separate, mutually contradictory versions of what Saionji had supposedly said, none of which matched the two words he'd actually heard her say, and corrected none of them, on the grounds that it wasn't his story to manage.

Somewhere in the retelling, the number itself had already stopped being the point for most people. It had become a story about Saionji, which made sense, since she was the one three years of reputation had been resting on. He was, in most versions, barely a character in it at all. A name. A footnote with good test-taking instincts. He found he didn't mind that either.

He lay awake longer than he should have that night, given he had a shift in six hours. He kept returning, uselessly, to the crack at the board, not the number, not the reputation, none of the parts of it Kaito clearly thought mattered. Just her face, doing the one thing it apparently wasn't supposed to do in public, before it remembered its job and stopped.

He put the evening away somewhere he wouldn't have to look at it directly, the way he put away most things that made him feel more than he'd planned on feeling, and told himself it was simply an exam, and the rest was noise.

He was, as it happened, going to be wrong about which part of that sentence mattered.
