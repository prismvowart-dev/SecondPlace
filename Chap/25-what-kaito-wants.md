# Chapter 25: What Kaito Wants

Kaito had been quiet for four days, which was itself the kind of information a person could build an investigation around, except that Itsuki already knew where to look.

He found the source on Tuesday evening, spread across Kaito's half of the room in a chaos that had, this time, a shape underneath it: three navy suits still in their plastic sleeves, a stack of pamphlets from career fairs Itsuki hadn't known were happening, and a laptop open to a form with more blank fields than Kaito appeared to have patience for.

"Don't," Kaito said, without looking up, "say anything about the suits."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were thinking it."

"I was thinking there are three of them." Itsuki set his bag down. "That seems like an unnecessary number of identical suits."

"They're not identical. They're subtly different shades of the same color, which the internet assures me is a meaningful distinction." Kaito dropped his head back against the wall and stared at the ceiling with the exhaustion of someone who had been staring at variations of the same form for several hours. "I have to write four hundred characters about why I want to intern at a company that makes industrial packaging. Four hundred. I don't have forty characters of feelings about packaging, man. I don't have four."

"Then don't apply there."

"It closes on the thirtieth. It's the early window." Kaito rubbed his face with both hands. "Everyone says the actual hiring is next spring, but the companies run an intern pipeline in the autumn that decides who gets a seat at the real one. If you skip it, you're competing against everyone who didn't. My senpai says that's worse than applying cold."

"Everyone's applying everywhere. That's the system. You send forty sheets so that four turn into interviews, and the sheet is the only part they read before they decide if you're worth a room." Kaito let his hands fall. "You and Saionji have known what you're doing since the first day. Research track, scholarship, whatever board you two keep quietly fighting over that nobody else understands the rules of. I don't have a track. I have a faculty I picked because the building was nearer the gym."

Itsuki considered this the way he considered most things Kaito said that carried more weight than Kaito's delivery let on. "You picked Yui. Nobody made you do the reading on that one, and you didn't look lost."

"That's different."

"Why?"

Kaito opened his mouth, closed it, and the silence sat between them long enough for Itsuki to look over.

"I don't know," Kaito admitted, more quietly than his register usually allowed. "I just knew. Same way you knew about her, probably, except you're both insufferably disciplined about not saying so and I'm just insufferable." He gestured at the form. "This isn't like that. Nobody knows what they want out of a career at twenty. I don't know how the two of you always seem to."

"We don't." Itsuki pulled out his chair and turned it around to face Kaito's side of the room instead of his desk. "I know what keeps the scholarship alive. That's not the same thing as knowing what I want."

That seemed to land somewhere Kaito hadn't expected, and neither of them said anything for a while. The dormitory had settled into the quiet it got after ten, somebody down the hall running water, a door two rooms over closing more softly than the last.

"Show me the form," Itsuki said.

"Why, are you secretly passionate about industrial packaging?"

"No. But I've read a great many things I didn't care about and made them sound like I did. It's most of what a scholarship application is." He held out a hand. "Four hundred characters. Give me the draft you already deleted."

Kaito stared at him, then slowly pulled the laptop closer and turned it around.

___

It took most of an hour, and it was not an efficient use of one. Sentence by sentence, Itsuki cut what sounded like everyone else's application and kept the two lines that sounded like Kaito: the part about organizing three intramural leagues nobody had asked him to organize, and the part about being the person people called when a plan fell through. The rest he deleted without comment, line after line, the way a man clears a table. At some point his own sentence ended halfway, and he realized he was looking at the word *packaging* without the rest of the line behind it.

"Itsuki."

"Yes."

"You went."

"I didn't go anywhere."

"You said 'packaging' and then you were gone for about eight seconds. Your eyes were open." Kaito had set down the laptop. "When did you last sleep eight hours?"

"I don't remember."

"That's not a good sign, man."

"It evens out."

"That's what you said last term."

"It was true last term too." Itsuki pushed his glasses up with two fingers, noticed that he'd done it, and put his hand down.

Kaito read the finished draft twice, quietly, which for Kaito counted as a small ceremony.

"This is annoyingly good," he said.

"You wrote most of it. I removed the parts where you were pretending to be a man dressed by his parents for an interview."

"That's a pointed and accurate insult." Kaito shut the laptop, and some of the static went out of him. "You didn't have to do this."

"No."

"Then why did you."

Itsuki thought about it honestly, for a moment longer than he usually allowed himself to think about his own motives. "Because you did the same thing for me on the first day, without being asked. It took me two and a half years to return it in a form you'd accept."

Kaito had no joke ready, which happened rarely enough that Itsuki noted it, and said nothing further.

___

He worked that night with the form still in his head and his own tiredness behind it like a second voice. A little after one, at the register, he gave a customer the wrong change, and caught it only when the man had already turned toward the door.

"Sorry. Hold on."

He corrected it. It was the first mistake he had made at that counter in two years, and it was a small one, a hundred yen, and the man didn't even seem to mind. Itsuki stood there afterward with the till drawer open and the wrong coin still warm in his hand.

His phone buzzed on the counter just before three. **Reina:** *My grandfather's assistant asked me for the seminar roster this afternoon. Routine, apparently.*

He read it twice. He typed *noted* and then deleted it, and typed it again.

He did not ask what was routine about it.
