# Chapter 9: A Defensible Margin

It happened on a Thursday, eight weeks into the arrangement, on a night when his shift the day before had run long because the evening clerk called in sick and the manager hadn't found a replacement before Itsuki's own shift started.

He was midway through a sentence of the draft when it simply stopped. He'd been building toward this paragraph for twenty minutes, something about the incentive buried inside the funding formula. His pen rolled off the edge of the notebook. His forehead came down onto his forearm, and his glasses tipped forward against the spine of the book underneath it.

Saionji noticed the pen stop before she noticed anything else about it. She looked up from her own page, took in the shape of him, folded over, one hand still holding the pencil case in place out of habit, breathing slow, and didn't say his name.

She got up instead, without a sound, walked around behind him, and turned the green-shaded lamp so its light fell away from his face rather than across it. Then she sat back down and kept working, and didn't touch his half of the draft, not even to fix the citation she'd clearly already spotted was wrong.

He woke to a small, precise click. She had set her steel ruler down on the table from a height of exactly one centimeter, which he understood, once he was fully conscious, to be a deliberate compromise between waking him gently and not waking him at all.

"It's nine twenty," she said, without looking up from her screen. "You said ten."

"How long was I—"

"Forty minutes." She turned a page. "I judged that a defensible margin."

There was a crease down the side of his face from where it had pressed against the notebook's spine. He was fairly sure she'd seen it and equally sure she wasn't going to mention it. She slid the coffee across the table, the one she never thanked him for, still faintly warm, and went back to her pen without another word on the subject.

He sat with that for a while, doing nothing useful with the time. Forty minutes gone from a night that had no minutes to spare, and she had spent every one of them not waking him, not fixing his half of the draft, not saying anything he could have thanked her for even if he'd wanted to. He didn't know what to do with a kindness that came with no opening to return it. He drank the coffee instead, and picked up his pen, and let the rest of the evening go on as if none of it had happened, because that was clearly what she'd decided it was going to be.

___

They submitted the finished draft four hours before the deadline, which by her standards counted as reckless improvisation and by his counted as exactly on schedule. She read the final version over twice, found nothing wrong with it, and seemed, if anything, faintly disappointed about that.

"That's it, then," she said, closing the laptop. "Until the revisions come back."

"That's it."

They both stayed at the table a moment longer than the moment required.

The bulletin board outside the seminar building had a new flyer up by the time they left, bright and slightly too enthusiastic in its layout: *END-OF-TERM SPORTS FESTIVAL.* A sign-up sheet dangled beneath it on a length of string, already half full.

Itsuki didn't think much of it at the time. He had no way of knowing yet that Kaito had found the same sheet an hour earlier, and had already decided, with the confidence of a man who considered other people's futures a shared resource, exactly whose names were going on it.
